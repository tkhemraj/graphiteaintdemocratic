The legal framework problem

This is not about where Paragon is based.
It is about jurisdiction, data residency, and due process.

Any entity — regardless of country of origin — that collects, processes, or retains personal data about an individual should be required to have legitimate legal authority over that person, a lawful basis for collection, and data residency obligations that keep the data within a jurisdiction where the individual has enforceable rights. Commercial spyware operated through third-country infrastructure is engineered to circumvent all three. That is the problem.

Fourth Amendment

Only constrains US government actors

When a foreign government uses Graphite on an American journalist, the Fourth Amendment does not apply. The collection is legally invisible to US constitutional law. Data can be shared with other foreign agencies — no US judicial oversight at any point in the chain.

CLOUD Act (2018)

Governs US companies — not foreign spyware operators

The CLOUD Act can compel Google or Apple to produce data stored abroad. It has no mechanism to compel a foreign spyware company. When Paragon's infrastructure receives exfiltrated data from an American's device, no US law requires Paragon to preserve, disclose, or delete it. No notification right exists.

No federal data residency law

The United States has no GDPR equivalent

Privacy practitioners who spent years ensuring GDPR data residency compliance for clients — keeping EU personal data within jurisdictions where data subjects have enforceable rights — have no equivalent framework protecting their own data as Americans. That gap is structural, not accidental.

GDPR — National security carve-out

Even the EU's gold standard has a gap

GDPR Article 2(2) explicitly excludes national security activities from its scope. When Italy uses Graphite on Italian journalists, GDPR does not apply. Israel has an EU adequacy decision — but it covers commercial data transfers, not intelligence collection through third-party spyware infrastructure.

Sector laws (HIPAA, CCPA, ECPA)

Govern custodians — not data that was stolen

HIPAA, CCPA, and ECPA govern companies holding your data as part of a service relationship. None of them govern data covertly exfiltrated by state-sponsored spyware. If Graphite copies your protected health records off your phone, HIPAA provides no remedy — the data was not obtained from a covered entity.

What lawful collection requires

The standard that should apply

Collection should require: judicial authorization from a court with jurisdiction; a documented lawful basis; data stored within a jurisdiction where the subject has rights; retention limited to the stated purpose; and the subject's ability to challenge the collection. Graphite satisfies none of these for any of its documented targets.

Quickstart

Check your device

gaid runs locally — no data leaves your machine. Android requires USB debugging enabled. iOS backup analysis works on any standard iTunes/Finder backup. 

# Install
$ pip install git+https://github.com/tkhemraj/graphiteaintdemocratic

# Android — scan live logcat via ADB (device connected + USB debugging on)
$ gaid android --adb
[*] Pulling logcat from connected device...
[!!!] FOUND 'BIGPRETZEL' [HIGH] — uniquely identifies Graphite infection

# Android — scan a saved bugreport
$ adb bugreport bugreport.zip
$ gaid android --bugreport bugreport.zip

# iOS — analyze iTunes backup
$ gaid ios --backup ~/Library/Application\ Support/MobileSync/Backup/<udid>

# iOS — analyze sysdiagnose archive (Settings → Privacy → Analytics)
$ gaid ios --sysdiagnose sysdiagnose.tar.gz

# iOS — check shutdown log for process anomalies
$ gaid ios --shutdown-log /path/to/shutdown.log

# Network — analyze a PCAP for C2 communication
$ gaid network --pcap capture.pcap

# Show loaded IOC summary
$ gaid iocs
Loaded IOCs:
  Domains:         11
  IPs:             9
  Log strings:     3
    [HIGH] BIGPRETZEL  (android) — uniquely identifies Graphite infection
    [MEDIUM] SMALLPRETZEL  (ios) — implausible CloudKit/appleaccountd activity
